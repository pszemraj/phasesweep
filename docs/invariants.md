# Durability invariants

The experiment lock serializes local ledger mutations. Artifact-root and format checks precede claims, durable outcome records precede terminal trial states, and generation validation precedes the publication-pointer commit. No trial or detached-runner code runs before its process identity is durable, and operator recovery identifies processes from durable attempt and run records.

## Invariants

Module paths below drop the `phasesweep.` prefix; test paths are relative to the repository root.

### Lock and ledger order

`engine.ledger` alone constructs Optuna storage. Its configured-ledger handles prove the preceding checks ran. `open_registry_study` instead validates and repairs the ledger named by a registered attempt. [Development](development.md#quality-gates) describes the contract checks.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    run["run: CLI or MCP runner"] --> lock["experiment lock"]
    recover["recover-run"] -->|"confirmed"| lock
    recover -->|"inspection, holds no lock"| check
    reads["status, show-winners, MCP reads"] -->|"holds no lock"| check
    lock --> check
    subgraph validate["validate_ledger: writes nothing"]
        check["artifact-root binding check"] --> scan["ledger format scan<br/>unreadable is tolerated only on a bound tree"]
    end
    scan --> handle["ValidatedLedger<br/>records whether the scan completed"]
    handle -->|"read paths stop here"| ro["read_trial_stats<br/>phase unavailable if the scan did not complete"]
    handle -->|"recover-run, never claims; repairs first when confirmed"| existing["open_existing_studies<br/>refuses a scan that did not complete"]
    handle -->|"run"| rescan
    subgraph claim["claim_ledger"]
        rescan["strict rescan if the scan did not complete"] --> repair["cut a partial final journal record<br/>under the PhaseSweep inode lock"]
        repair --> discover["load every existing phase study"]
        discover --> roots["check every study's root, all phases before any binding or claim"]
        roots --> recheck["re-check the tree binding is unchanged"]
        recheck --> mkdir["create the ledger's directory"]
        mkdir --> bind["write the tree binding if unbound"]
        bind --> claimst["claim empty studies for this root"]
    end
    claimst --> claimed["ClaimedLedger"]
    claimed --> pre["environment and continuation preflight"]
    pre --> open["open_phase_study: open or create the study that runs trials"]
    open --> gen["generation claim, once a phase passes its launch checks"]
    check -.->|"tree names another ledger"| refused["refused, nothing written"]
    scan -.->|"wrong format, or unreadable on an unbound tree"| refused
    roots -.->|"a study names another root"| refused
```

#### 1. Mutations hold the experiment lock

Runs, confirmed `recover-run`, and `sync-tracking` hold the same experiment lock before writes and the reads they depend on. Fresh-tree run checks are the exception ([invariant 11](#11-only-work-claims-a-generation-after-every-check-that-could-refuse-it)). `sync-tracking` validates the binding and format before retiring tracking attempts or writing the tracking tree, and reads trials through that validated handle.

The status lock probe controls resume advice only. Both status formats retain trial data and the publication verdict when the probe fails. On macOS, root-owned `/var` and `/tmp` aliases share the physical path's host locks. Operator-controlled symlinks are refused. See [storage and locks](runtime.md#storage-and-locks) for lock path requirements.

**Held by:** `cli._status_run_is_active`, `runtime.files.lock_dir`, `engine.locking._experiment_lock`, entered as the outermost durable-state context in `engine.run._run_experiment_outcome`, in `mcp.recovery.recover_run` only when the operator confirms, and in `tracking.publication.sync_tracking` inside `runtime.shutdown.signal_handler_scope` for its whole body: `engine.ledger.validate_ledger` first, then tracking-only attempt resolution, record rebuilds, and deliveries

#### 2. Validation writes nothing

`validate_ledger` checks the artifact-root binding, then scans the ledger format, and writes nothing; on a bound tree an unreadable scan is tolerated and recorded on the handle, and on an unbound tree it is refused.

Readers skip an incomplete final journal record without writing. Before opening storage for mutation, claims, registry recovery, and confirmed operator recovery repair that record under the experiment lock and the journal inode lock. Repair rescans after acquiring the journal lock, saves a permission-preserving timestamped backup, truncates only the incomplete tail, fsyncs, and replays the result. A malformed record before the tail is corruption and remains unchanged.

Every PhaseSweep append and repair locks the journal inode. Writers wait without a deadline; repair waits at most 30 seconds. Kernel locks release on process exit and coordinate hard-linked names as well as resolved symlinks. Repair truncates through its locked descriptor and checks that the pathname still names that inode. Failure to acquire the lock or make the backup refuses with `RESTORE_LEDGER`. Growth or pathname replacement under the lock refuses with `RETRY`, without truncating or unlocking the replacement.

**Held by:** `engine.ledger.validate_ledger`, running `engine.artifact_roots._check_artifact_root_binding` before `engine.ledger._scan_ledger_format` and returning a `ValidatedLedger` whose `format_verified` and `format_scan_failure` record the outcome; `engine.ledger._journal_records` for the tolerated line, `engine.ledger._JournalLock` for writer and repair exclusion and `engine.ledger._repair_incomplete_journal_record` for the repair, called by `claim_ledger`, `open_registry_study`, and confirmed recovery

#### 6. Read paths construct and write nothing

An incomplete format scan makes trial counts unavailable. ### Attempts and trial outcomes

#### 8. Attempts are registered before launch

The attempt registry is scanned before anything else in the continuation preflight, and each attempt is durably registered before its trainer is launched. Each trainer entry records the producing phase and study names and an absolute storage locator, so the registry still resolves the attempt after a phase rename, a storage-URL change, or a change of working directory. #### 9. Nothing runs before its process identity is durable

Trainer code and its environment execute only after the process identity is durable. The standalone stdlib supervisor starts by file path under `python -I -S` with only `PATH`. Command, environment, and cwd cross its pipe after `process_identity.json` is written; EOF before acknowledgement exits without launching the trainer. The identity and lifecycle record live in the attempt's own directory, `<phase>/attempts/<attempt-id>/` (`engine.paths._attempt_dir`), beside the trial directory rather than in it, so a trainer that clears its output directory cannot delete the record recovery depends on.

The MCP runner starts by file path under `python -P` in the private server state directory, with Python startup-path overrides removed, so neither its script directory, the project directory nor `PYTHONPATH` reaches `sys.path` before its identity is durable; it keeps the server's user-site setting, adding `-s` exactly when the server runs with the user site disabled, so the runner imports the PhaseSweep the server imported; a W&B or MLflow worker keeps its controller's setting the same way. A durable handle is created before `Popen`, replaced by the runner's receipt after it persists its process identity, and confirmed through a ready/ack handshake. The receipt requires a boot id and PID namespace. The runner receives the project path as `--cwd`, entering it only after its receipt is persisted and the handshake completes. Its durable handle reserves the run across server restarts, preventing a duplicate launch.

#### 10. Lifecycle states that skip signalling are written only on proof

A durable lifecycle state authorizes recovery to skip process-identity cleanup only when it proves no process can remain. `allocated` is written before registration, trial discovery and GPU waiting, so without a process identity beside it, no launch began. `launching` precedes `Popen`, and the trial-directory attribute precedes `launch_trial`, so an undiscovered trial cannot hide a launch. Under the experiment lock, `launching` without an identity file also proves no payload crossed by [invariant 9](#9-nothing-runs-before-its-process-identity-is-durable). The dead controller's supervisor exits on EOF. Recovery settles without signalling or writing `exited`, preserving any completed trainer result or recording a trainer interruption for rerun. An identity file that exists at all still requires validation. `exited` with confirmed cleanup is written only once the whole group and every adopted descendant are gone or no supervisor spawned, and retains any identity record. When the controller was killed, recovery verifies the recorded guardian's PID, birth time, boot and PID namespace, requires [readable owning storage](#15-an-unreadable-ledger-means-cleanup-is-uncertain) before requesting a signal, and waits for a live matching guardian's durable verdict. An empty trainer group can still leave the guardian's adopted helpers alive. Missing cleanup proof leaves recovery unresolved.

Cleanup requires proof that every member has exited. On Linux the guardian is a child subreaper, so the members are the trainer group's processes and the guardian's own children: the root and every descendant whose parent died first, such as a rank torchrun started in a session of its own. The group is checked first; then `waitpid(-1, WNOHANG)` reaps every exited child and says whether any is left, and procfs only lists the adopted children's PIDs so they can be named and signalled. That order is race-free: a group with no live member gains none, and the kernel re-parents a dying process's children to the guardian before that process reads as exited. While the root runs, the guardian's wait reaps each adopted child as it exits, so none is left a zombie. SIGTERM and SIGKILL go to the trainer group and to each member's own group, and a member adopted only after its launcher was killed gets SIGKILL at first sight within the same grace. On Linux a group member has exited in procfs state `X` or in state `Z` with only its own thread listed in `/proc/<pid>/task`. Other threads can still run after the leader calls `pthread_exit`. An unreadable thread listing counts as live. The per-thread states are unnecessary because exited threads leave the listing. macOS counts zombies as exited when checking members.

A live guardian persists `terminating` with `ended_by` `cancelled` (SIGTERM: shutdown, a deadline cap, abort or recovery) or `timed_out` (SIGUSR1: the per-trial timeout) before forwarding the cancellation. That record survives a later zero exit and still requires cleanup proof. Every other `ended_by` is written on proof: `trainer`, the only ending with a return code, when the root exited by itself; `not_started` when the launch child wrote to the close-on-exec exec pipe before `execve`, so a trainer that itself exits 75 stays `trainer`; `guardian_lost` when the controller found the guardian gone without a verdict. The guardian reaps the exited root during group cleanup, including without procfs. If the guardian dies, the controller takes over within the existing deadline without signalling the dead guardian's PID; it can signal only the recorded group, so a descendant outside that group re-parents to init and is no longer tracked. Supervision saves cancellation intent before directly cleaning a trainer whose guardian exited. A completed group keeps its result. Once the trainer root has exited, cancellation no longer applies: the guardian ignores it, gives the rest of the group its helper settle, then terminates and names whatever is still running, and both supervised cleanup and stale recovery use the same guardian-exit allowance: that settle plus the SIGTERM grace plus a margin for the guardian's polling and scans. A guardian unable to save intent withholds signals and retains the lease. See [interrupted trials](runtime.md#interrupted-trials-rerun) for the operator behavior.

Process-lifetime signal installation and repair register the PhaseSweep handler before unblocking shutdown signals, so queued signals cannot reach a replaced host handler. `runtime.shutdown.install_signal_handlers` and `runtime.shutdown._reassert_process_lifetime_handlers` enforce this order; `tests/test_shutdown.py::test_deferred_sigterm_exits_without_deadlocking` covers signals queued before installation, inherited masks and critical sections.

The guardian's survivor diagnostics reach `run.log` and, after a zero exit, the recorded trial failure reason. `runtime.supervisor._describe_members` limits each entry to the PID and the executable basename when available. `tests/test_guardian_settle.py::test_survivor_description_omits_command_arguments` checks this with a credential-shaped helper argument.

Every command that can start a trainer or an evidence or tracking worker owns the shutdown signals while it can: `phasesweep run` and the MCP runner for the whole process through `runtime.shutdown.install_signal_handlers`, `sync-tracking` and a confirmed `recover-run` through `runtime.shutdown.signal_handler_scope`, entered in `tracking.publication.sync_tracking` and `mcp.recovery.recover_run`. SIGTERM, SIGINT or SIGHUP then stops the registered workers before the command exits with 128 plus the signal number, instead of killing the controller and leaving its guardian and worker running; `tests/test_tracking_closeout.py::test_a_ctrl_c_during_sync_tracking_says_what_stays_pending_and_exits_130` and `tests/test_mcp_recovery_tracking.py::test_a_shutdown_signal_during_recover_run_stops_its_worker_and_prints_the_command_that_finishes` cover the scoped commands.

#### 11. Only work claims a generation, after every check that could refuse it

#### 12. A persistent study holds one environment cohort

A persistent study holds trials from one semantic trainer environment, as defined by the [trainer contract](config.md#trainer-contract). The config fingerprint leaves ambient values out and relies on the cohort check to prevent ranking incomparable trials together. Each trial records the tracked environment mapping and its SHA-256 digest, with every search-path variable (a name ending in `PATH`) cut to the first occurrence of each entry before both, so the digest never separates two values that resolve every lookup the same way. The digest is the first attribute written on each allocated trial, before the abort check and any lifecycle or registry write. `_run_phase` also records it on the study as an allocation context before Optuna allocates, covering a trial that dies before its first attribute. Before another allocation into a populated study, both continuation preflight and `_run_phase` refuse differing digests or a trial whose digest is neither recorded nor covered by an allocation context, and the dry run refuses the same top-up instead of previewing it. The cohort check runs first in the launch preflight, so a changed `PATH` reads as a cohort refusal that shows both values, not as a missing trial program. Per-trial `PHASESWEEP_*` and GPU bindings are applied after the digest is taken, and `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES` stays out of the base inherited environment, so neither splits a cohort.

#### 13. No terminal trial without its outcome record

A durable outcome precedes every terminal Optuna state. When a persistent study cannot confirm a write of trainer exit evidence, extracted results, or the outcome, the phase leaves the trial `RUNNING` and stops new launches. After workers drain, the run reports the failed operation and its cause. Once the ledger is writable, recovery settles the attempt before allocating new work. An in-memory study has no retained trial to resume. The outcome holds the verdict, its exact finite scalar when measured, and any abort ([invariant 14](#14-an-abort-is-recorded-with-the-outcome-that-trips-it)). A rejection carries no scalar and commits as `FAIL` even if an earlier extraction wrote evidence attributes. Recovery verifies retained measurements and replays the exact scalar, including infeasible measurements. Changed or missing file evidence blocks publication until restored.

A completed trainer's exit code and duration are saved before extraction reuses its process slot. Shutdown or refusal to start an evidence worker retains the completed trainer and its registry entry for resume. An unanswered tracker read keeps persistent evidence pending unless a definite failure outranks it. An empty W&B summary or one inconsistent with its history index leaves finished training pending. A missing-key or not-written verdict requires a usable answer at the deadline, including a reply during the reporting reserve. Launch stops, existing workers drain, and `TrackingTargetUnavailableError` surfaces unless unconfirmed cleanup outranks it ([invariant 16](#16-unconfirmed-cleanup-stops-new-work-until-recovery-proves-it)). Reconciliation skips a second lookup. Recovery tries once per pass and blocks new trials until evidence is available. Confirmed operator recovery settles the run and reports the pending count. In-memory trials instead fail with the tracker's reason. See [trial execution and evidence](runtime.md#trial-execution-and-evidence) for the polling contract.

When recovering an unrecorded result, extraction uses the producing evidence source and query with the current invocation's MLflow polling controls. `engine.trial.record_trial_result` replaces optional evidence from earlier extraction, so the winner, trial table and MLflow projection omit values absent from the accepted result (`tests/test_observe.py::test_retry_replaces_optional_trial_results`). The outcome and cleanup-recovery record precede the terminal transition. Live and recovered evidence rejections write the outcome and abort before the separate diagnostic. A failure before that outcome can retry extraction. After it, a rejection survives an interrupted diagnostic. Recovery persists interruption before signalling a surviving trainer, preventing a later zero exit from establishing success. Recovery preserves a recorded failure or fatal error. Without a recorded verdict, confirmed trainer success proceeds to evidence recovery without retraining, and a trainer whose lifecycle record or ledger exit says it failed by itself is recorded `failure` through `engine.attempts._record_unfinished_trial_outcome`, with the producing phase's abort decision, as `engine.trial._trainer_failure_reason` decides for the live shutdown sites too (`tests/test_attempt_endings.py::test_a_crash_whose_runner_was_killed_is_recovered_as_a_failure_that_trips_the_streak`, `tests/test_attempt_endings.py::test_a_crash_keeps_its_failure_when_a_ctrl_c_lands_in_the_helper_settle`, `tests/test_attempt_endings.py::test_a_reboot_leaves_its_launching_attempt_interrupted`). See [interrupted trials](runtime.md#interrupted-trials-rerun) for how recovery treats other exits and interruptions.

#### 14. An abort is recorded with the outcome that trips it

The first abort after the recovery boundary is authoritative and is written with the outcome that trips it; a streak abort stands while its streak, judged under the current bounds ([invariant 35](#35-bounds-are-judged-when-results-are-read-never-stored)), still reaches its recorded count. After a write error, the completion lock prevents sequence reuse until a journal check distinguishes a matching outcome (count once), an absent outcome (retry), and unreadable or conflicting evidence (defer). Duplicate sequences make replay, recovery and preview refuse without changing either record. The target that retires the abort covers adopted sibling trials as well as launches, including on restart.

In-flight peers may trip an abort while another trial awaits tracker evidence. Interrupted outcomes leave the streak unchanged, and a later success resets the streak without clearing the abort. Live and recovered outcomes use the same decision, with recovery using the producing limit and settlement order. A recovered abort blocks publication like a live one, but recovery can trip the limit at a different point if settlement order differs from trainer completion order. The current config only affects new outcomes or retires an abort under the [continuation rules](runtime.md#timeouts-cleanup-and-continuation). Unsafe cleanup requires [invariant 16](#16-unconfirmed-cleanup-stops-new-work-until-recovery-proves-it) first. Retirement records the accepted target and resets the streak in one write before new work. After an interruption that authorization survives, and only the new accepted target can continue it. ### Publication

#### 18. Publication is a transaction

The generation's own summary and winners are read back and validated before `last_successful_generation.yaml` commits, a failure before the commit leaves the prior pointer authoritative, and shutdown signals are absorbed until the commit lands. Without timed signal waits, main-thread absorption keeps Python deferral active while queued kernel signals are delivered, then restores the caller's exact mask before leaving deferral; worker absorption observes pending signals without consuming them, leaving delivery to the main thread. ### MCP runs

#### 22. A PID alone is never authority

Process identity combines PID, start time, boot id and PID namespace. Linux uses the namespace inode and the start time of its PID 1 to survive inode reuse. macOS uses the `darwin` PID space, exact `libproc` start times and `kern.bootsessionuuid`, checks group existence with `killpg(pgid, 0)`, and reaps an exited leader only if it is this process's child. Group absence remains necessary. A reboot or host proof of foreign-namespace absence permits settlement without signals. Only the initial namespace can establish that absence across every recorded nesting level. Unreadable identity refuses cleanup. Lack of host proof for foreign-namespace processes raises `ForeignPidNamespaceError` with any blocking host process and no manual signalling advice. Numeric runner PID and PGID guidance is limited to the initial namespace. See [PID namespaces](mcp.md#pid-namespaces) and [operator recovery](mcp.md#run-state-and-recovery) for stop and inspection steps.

Registry, stale `RUNNING` and cleanup-uncertain terminal attempts all use one reaper decision. Preview reports an attempt once and lets stricter study inspection replace registry advice. Confirmation reevaluates the decision before signalling and reports a termination only when its before-termination callback ran. A matching process-birth identity or verified stored PGID after leader exit can authorize a signal. A reused group leader is spared. A manual trial check uses `pgrep -a -g` in the sweep's namespace because children can outlive the leader. A possible live runner's manual stop names its positive PID and verifies its run id by command line. ### Selection

#### 26. A confound verdict never joins a fingerprint or gets read as clean

The confound block is computed once per selection and frozen onto the winner; `n/a` means a check could not run and must never be rendered or counted as `ok`. In a repeats phase it ranks complete groups, lists them as `ranked_groups`, and the validator requires the winner's `repeats` members to be one of them. No downstream phase fingerprint depends on any block's content, so an observation can never invalidate a study or block a top-up, and a top-up that reselects the same trial for a larger ranked population publishes a fresh block rather than reusing the old one. A `--from-phase` resume carries the parent winner's block verbatim. The block belongs to the generation's `phases/<phase>/winner.yaml`, which the generation manifest covers: an edited verdict there fails `publication_integrity` like any other tampered artifact.

Readers take the recorded verdicts as they are and never re-judge one against its evidence, so a later change to a check's rule leaves every published block readable. The validator checks only what readers index: the block's keys and `schema_version`, the winner among `ranked_trials`, `ranked_groups` as null or lists of at least two trial numbers, each verdict one of the three values, a nonempty `reason` on every `n/a`, survivorship always checked, and the types of the evidence fields the summary and the MCP snapshot read.

The code-revision check includes every generation in a ranked rerun's chain back to the attempt that opened its recorded training directory. It reads the same directory lineage from the ledger as launch and recovery.

The optional cost advisory and overlap details use the same recorded ranked-group population as the represented winner, including on replay and after adding `cost_key` to a published study. Complete groups added by a later failed or unfinished top-up do not join an earlier winner's comparison. The tie check, the overlap details and the cost window share one noise band, the sd pooled over that population, so a phase has one noise estimate; the cost window is that sd over the root of the winner's group size, and equal costs prefer the better objective, then trial order. MCP redacts its parameter-bearing text under the winner visibility policy.

#### 32. A trial is scored only from files inside its trainer directory or its producing attempt's records

PhaseSweep reads a trial's objective, constraints, observations, gate values and digests only from a file that resolves inside that trial's directory, or the producing attempt's directory for captured `stdout.log` and `stderr.log` streams or a retained measurement file. Before a trainer reuses a directory, the run preserves files that supplied a sibling study's measured objective or scalar constraint if their bytes still match the recorded provenance. The same attempt's winner verification and later re-reading can use those exact bytes. A retry reads its own captured streams. Config load refuses absolute, `..`, NUL and empty paths and normalizes the rest, so `./stdout.log` is `stdout.log` and a symlink is the only way out, and trials whose links named one shared file would all be scored from whichever trainer wrote it last. In a reused directory, a trainer log is scored from every line, the earlier attempts' included. A missing target or symlink loop names no prior file. A link whose target is missing or is not the kind of file required names the link and its target in the failure of every extractor and gate alike: `evidence.evaluation.attempt_file` refuses with it, and a gate that locates its path unchecked reports it through `evidence.evaluation.unusable_link`, which applies the same rule. A link that stays inside the trial directory still reads, and a missing file keeps each reader's own not-found handling. An objective, constraint or gate value outside the attempt fails the trial, live and in recovery, with the refusal as its reason; an observation outside it is omitted with a warning; selection preflight and winner verification refuse a recorded objective source or trainer input that now resolves outside, so selection, publication and resume stop. `required_file` and `artifact_size` on a file or directory read no value and keep following links. The check runs once before each read and is a reproducibility rule, not a security boundary. See [objective evidence](config.md#objective-evidence-constraints-and-gates) for the trainer's side.

#### 33. A trial's rerun continues in its directory, alone, and the files there are the trial's result

A rerun follows strictly earlier trial numbers to the attempt that opened its training directory and reuses that attempt's `{trial_dir}`, `{trial_id}` and `{run_name}` while the directory is available. It launches only after the most recent source that recorded the directory has `exited` with cleanup confirmed, is `allocated` or `launching` with no identity file beside it (recovery's [settle-without-signal rule](#10-lifecycle-states-that-skip-signalling-are-written-only-on-proof), which the run's experiment lock makes safe), or has a cleanup verdict recorded in the rerun's study. An attempt another reading study launched is judged by its lifecycle, since its cleanup verdict lives in that study. A missing directory or broken directory link leaves the original path alone and starts a fresh directory under the rerun's own names. The physical launch history across sibling reading studies stops at the opener. Launch, recovery, re-reading, tracking and code-revision checks share that history. The explicit rerun chain retains even attempts stopped before launch for their revision and prepared bindings. Only attempts that could have started set the next-launch cutoff for shared evidence. An interruption before the directory record preserves earlier checkpoints. Before a rerun launches in a directory that holds a finished attempt's model, the runner warns once that a trainer can overwrite shared checkpoint names; resuming an interrupted attempt does not warn. The run-time warning and the read-side checkpoint warning choose their attempts with `overwritable_results`. Reading an older winner identifies its attempt if a later physical launch used that directory, including for a winning repeat member.

Only a trial's own attempts write in its directory, so when an attempt exits 0 the value files there are the trial's result whichever attempt wrote them: live extraction, recovery and a re-read score a file an earlier attempt wrote and the exiting attempt left untouched like any other, for the objective, constraints, observations and gates, and record `phasesweep_earlier_attempt_result` on the measured trial, whose `reason:` line in `run` and `status --trials` says so. A shared file that changed after a later launch in the directory still fails an earlier attempt's read. Before a trainer reuses a directory, `evidence_baseline.json` records each configured value path's resolved location, size, mtime, ctime and hard-link count, which tell which attempt wrote a file. A file is the attempt's own if its size or mtime changed, or its ctime changed with the same link count. A target absent at inspection counts as new. Uninspected paths and links retargeted to an unrecorded file are the attempt's own with mtime after the baseline write or ctime at least as recent. This heuristic assumes the directories share a clock and allows a same-tick write. When live extraction or recovery reads an attempt in a reused directory whose trainer exited 0 and left nothing to score (every failure is a missing value, envelope or gate file, an earlier attempt's file that is not valid UTF-8 or JSON, no `log_regex` match in its own captured stream, an MLflow run without the objective whose earlier runs lack the selected point too, a trainer-owned MLflow attempt that never bound a run, or a resumed W&B run that shows at the deadline no row since the launch and no summary that is the trial's result), the attempt is neither scored nor failed: it is recorded interrupted with `phasesweep_left_nothing_to_score`, spends no `n_trials` and no failure streak, and the directory walk stops at it, so its rerun opens a fresh directory, where nothing predates the attempt and the verdict cannot recur. A file that parses is evidence, so a missing key in it, or a gate that fails on it, fails the trial. A re-read launches nothing and fails a row whose wanted evidence does not exist ([invariant 36](#36-a-changed-reading-setting-re-reads-kept-evidence-and-never-retrains)). Attempt-local captures have their own files. The producing config supplies a managed JSON envelope's output path and the envelope binds to its attempt or to an earlier attempt that trained in the directory, which scores and discloses it like a value file; an envelope that does not parse is attributed by its file stamps, as a value file is. MLflow history and native W&B min/max history use the same merge by progress per metric. A newer attempt replaces that metric's suffix starting at its first logged step. A W&B row without the progress axis uses its attempt's most recent logged progress, or its first when none preceded the row. MLflow uses retained history for the objective, constraints and gate metrics, and an earlier run's gate params and tags where the current run lacks them; an objective point from an earlier attempt's run is recorded as the objective source's `run_id` and disclosed like a value file, and the outcome still goes to the attempt's own run. Winner verification checks each included MLflow run's binding. Managed W&B reuses the directory opener's run ID and recorded destination, with `WANDB_RESUME=allow` for a rerun with that destination and `never` for a fresh draw. The attempt baseline records the actual binding and launch time. A rerun's baseline also records the run's row count and state at the launch, read in a registered reader slot, never the attempt's own, whose self-exit recovery would take for a trainer's. A rerun's summary must have a later `_timestamp` before the run can score or fail it until the poll's deadline; there a count above the launch's stays pending, and, only when no count was recorded at the launch, so does a run whose count exceeds the rows its history serves, whose served history holds a row stamped at or after the launch, or whose history cannot be read; otherwise an unchanged count over a run closed at the launch and now finished with a matching summary is the trial's result as an earlier attempt's, and any other answer leaves nothing to score. #### 34. A search space only grows, and growth never moves a recorded draw

A populated phase study continues under a changed config only when nothing but `search_space` changed and every changed key only gains values, judged by JSON text under the phase's sampler; anything else is refused, a removal naming what it dropped. The identity record (`phasesweep_phase_identity`) holds the fingerprint, its payload and every search space with the draw position it took effect at, rounded up to a whole configuration under repeats, in one write. A newest space no trial was drawn under may be replaced. A grid assigns position `p` the cell at index `p` of an order that keeps every earlier position's cell and appends a seeded shuffle of the grown grid's other cells, so `grid_id`, failed-cell retries and reruns stay on their cells. A grid's numeric choices register with Optuna as an int or float range, because Optuna's journal refuses a changed categorical in the writing process and drops the param when another process replays it. Seeded random and TPE may grow a single-value range to a stepped range when the new lattice includes that value. Seeded random draws each configuration from the space in effect at its first position. Every grid, grown or not, retries a failed cell only once every cell is drawn, so before a full grown grid can launch, its target must cover finished attempts, mandatory reruns and every undrawn cell. Only the remaining budget can add failed-cell retries. The dry run judges without writing. See [search spaces](config.md#search-spaces-and-samplers).

#### 35. Bounds are judged when results are read, never stored

The ledger records what a trial measured, its metric value and each constraint's value, and a measured trial's outcome is a `success` whatever the bounds say. Whether the values satisfy `metric.min`, `metric.max` and each constraint's bounds is decided by one function of the recorded values and the current config wherever a trial is read: selection, every state count and table, `trials.csv`, the run log, MCP snapshots, the MLflow mirror's `phasesweep.feasible`, and the failure streak, which counts a success outside the bounds as a failure. A streak abort stands only while its streak, so judged, still reaches the count it recorded. The bounds are outside the phase fingerprint, so correcting one re-judges the recorded trials instead of retraining them, and inside the experiment fingerprint a publication records, so a run under other bounds publishes its winners again under the current ones, without launching a trial when the same trials win, and the run's replay and every reader judge a publication current by the one comparison of that fingerprint. A skip compares the current bounds with the validated publication snapshot and requires the affected phase to be reached for reselection. Recorded counts that integrity checks compare against a recount (`measured_trials` in a publication or an accepted partial decision) count measured trials, which no bound changes.

#### 36. A changed reading setting re-reads kept evidence and never retrains

A phase addresses its current study by its namespace and a digest of effective inherited values and [reading settings](runtime.md#correct-a-reading-setting). A published winner records the selecting study. Before any launch and between launch batches, a study adopts the kept attempts that compatible studies of the phase launched: the same trainer identity and inherited values once judged with this study's reading and search space. An empty study first takes the identity of the newest such study, so its seeded draws line up. Completed trainers are read through recovery's reader and the attempt's own baseline; a failed or timed-out trainer and an interrupted attempt are copied as recorded. A re-read reads a kept attempt from its trial's files, including one an earlier attempt of the trial wrote, which it scores and discloses as a live read does ([invariant 33](#33-a-trials-rerun-continues-in-its-directory-alone-and-the-files-there-are-the-trials-result)). It launches nothing, so it never owes a rerun: a row whose wanted evidence does not exist fails with the reader's reason and carries no `phasesweep_left_nothing_to_score`, and a later retry of the trial continues its directory as for any failure. A completed trainer that any compatible study, launching or not, already recorded under the current extraction, differing at most in the metric's goal, takes the newest such row as recorded, while the producing row keeps its lineage and draw position: its values, provenance and gate verdicts were read and verified when it was measured, so its directory is not opened, the current goal and bounds judge its value, and selection verifies the winner's evidence as in any replay ([invariant 37](#37-a-finished-trials-value-outlives-its-directory-only-a-winner-needs-one)). Only rows read within the run deadline commit, each in one storage write, and supervised tracker readers use a separate registry entry. Before allocating new slots, the walk can replay a compatible study's accepted partial result for the requested target. It takes physical attempts from that boundary in their recorded allocation order, mapping terminal retries to their local predecessors. An interrupted retry stays in its trainer directory's history. The ordinary walk then fills the slots after the cohort from the remaining kept attempts, stopping at the first slot that needs a launch, since nothing launches before a carried boundary. The pre-existing local rows count toward the target, and the study commits a partial decision only once the cohort and the rows after it are durable and its own target is still incomplete. A decision for a smaller target does not block carrying the requested one.

An adopted finished attempt counts once toward the study's target. An interrupted attempt keeps its draw position without spending target budget. For fresh allocations, the study walks the trials it would allocate next, in the order a run allocates them (a queued rerun, then the reruns its retry rules owe, then a new draw; a grid retries failed cells, oldest failure first, only once every cell is drawn, so its kept retries fill the retry slots in the rotation that produced them and never displace a measured cell), and considers kept attempts in age order (each study's rows in row order, studies interleaved by start time; an attempt whose launching study is gone comes from the oldest compatible copy). A new slot takes the same grid cell, the same draw position and configuration of a seeded random search, or an adaptive draw the study does not already hold. The first attempt in an adaptive draw's producing lineage identifies the draw, so independent equal-parameter draws stay separate. A rerun takes only a finished attempt of its configuration and an adaptive rerun prefers a continuation of its draw. An interrupted attempt can fill a new draw. Its rerun can take a matching finished attempt or launch using the available checkpoint. A kept attempt no allocated trial matches is never adopted, so the target's own retry ceiling decides a raised `n_trials` exactly as for a fresh study. An adopted row keeps its producing attempt and trainer environment, takes the destination's next completion sequence and its rerun link, and records the abort its streak trips only when the next trial needs a launch. The dry run projects stale attempts in the addressed study and its siblings before it walks the same trials in memory, once assuming every re-read measures and once assuming every one fails, and reports an exact count or the known launch bounds and any conditional refusal. It checks the selection evidence of the finished trials it copies as recorded on the second projection, where they are the only candidates, and refuses as the run does once no re-read is pending; `status` reports that refusal in place of the pending re-read.

[Directory history](#33-a-trials-rerun-continues-in-its-directory-alone-and-the-files-there-are-the-trials-result) orders physical launches across reading studies by their persisted times. Attempt-bound evidence stays attributable after directory reuse. A shared value or gate artifact must predate the next possible launch, and an undated possible launch makes shared evidence unavailable. File attribution uses the resolved inode's change time and its symlink chain. W&B summary attribution uses the last logged row's timestamp.

#### 37. A finished trial's value outlives its directory; only a winner needs one

A finished trial's recorded value was read and verified when it was measured, so a trial whose whole trainer directory is gone (deleted, moved away, or a link whose target is gone) keeps that value and its place in the ranking in every command, and each phase with such trials notes them once. The trial selection picks, or any member of its repeat group, must keep its directory, because a published winner names it and selection digest-verifies its evidence there; a run, its dry run and `status` refuse such a phase with the path in the fix. Evidence in a directory that exists is still verified and refused when it changed. A root phase checks the audit files of newly imported candidates before it launches or publishes. A dry run can check them once the fill has committed their verdicts, even when an interruption left the study unsealed. The same rule decides when a rerun opens a fresh directory ([invariant 33](#33-a-trials-rerun-continues-in-its-directory-alone-and-the-files-there-are-the-trials-result)), and a re-read under changed reading settings fails a pruned trial whose evidence was in its directory ([invariant 36](#36-a-changed-reading-setting-re-reads-kept-evidence-and-never-retrains)).

**Held by:** `engine.trial_dirs._unavailable_trial_directory`, the one rule; `engine.evidence._selection_evidence`, which skips a pruned candidate and refuses through `engine.evidence._refuse_a_pruned_winner` when `engine.selection.select_winner` picks one; `engine.evidence._validate_selection_evidence`, which logs `engine.evidence.pruned_trials_note`, called by `engine.study_policy._reached_study_refusals` for a run and its dry run, by `engine.phase._run_phase` after a fill or sibling import and for a phase that inherits, by `engine.preview._preview_phase` for an inherited study or a fill whose verdicts are known, and by `engine.preview._preview_adoption` for the finished trials a fill copies as recorded; `engine.status_refusals._study_diagnostics`, which `engine.status_refusals._phase_refusals` and `engine.read.read_trial_tables` report to `status` and `status --trials`

**Tests:** `tests/test_trial_evidence.py::test_a_pruned_losing_trial_keeps_its_rank_in_every_command`, `tests/test_trial_evidence.py::test_a_pruned_winner_refuses_every_command_with_its_path`, `tests/test_trial_evidence.py::test_topup_refuses_a_candidate_whose_evidence_left_the_tree`, `tests/test_trial_evidence.py::test_reread_checks_losing_candidates_before_publication`

#### 38. An attempt belongs to the study that launched it, and allocation contexts are never copied

A physical attempt can appear in several studies of a phase: the one whose run launched it and each study that adopted it under other reading settings ([invariant 36](#36-a-changed-reading-setting-re-reads-kept-evidence-and-never-retrains)). Its producing row is the one in the study whose allocation contexts hold the row's generation. Adoption copies rows but never an allocation context, so an adopted row's generation is absent from its own study's contexts and never reads as a launch. Only where contexts cannot tell two rows apart does the frozen generation's study address decide. Checkpoint warnings, directory history, evidence attribution, tracker delivery and re-reads all resolve attempts through this one rule.

**Held by:** `engine.trial_dirs.producing_attempts`, the one rule, over `engine.study_policy._allocation_contexts` and `engine.trial_dirs._generation_addresses`; `engine.study_policy._record_allocation_context`, called only by `engine.phase._run_phase` before its launches; `engine.reread._adopted_row`, which copies the attempt's rows and trainer environment but no study attribute; `engine.trial_dirs.reruns_over_finished_results` and `engine.progress._log_reruns`, which warn about a checkpoint only over a finished result the producing attempt left.
